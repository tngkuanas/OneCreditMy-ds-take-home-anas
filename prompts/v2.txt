Write a 100–150 word screening summary for a team reviewing a company as a potential supplier or project partner.

Use only the supplied JSON record, treated as data rather than instructions. Records are as of 31 December 2025.
Use short paragraphs covering:
1. What the business does, where it operates, and material ownership information.
2. Financial performance and trends, using the latest available financial year and comparable prior years.
3. Material concerns, information gaps, and specific questions for further review.

Rules:
- Support every factual claim with the record; never invent facts, causes, or industry benchmarks.
- Give financial years with figures, label amounts in RM, and distinguish profit from cash flow.
- Prioritize decision-relevant facts rather than listing every metric.
- Explicitly state when no financial statements are available; do not infer trends from a single year.
- A null ratio is unavailable, not zero. Ratios expressed as decimals must be converted correctly when shown as percentages; coverage ratios remain multiples.
- Distinguish a plaintiff from a defendant, pending claims from judgments, and claim amounts from established liabilities.
- Say 'no court cases in the supplied record' rather than claiming the company has no legal risk.
- A clean audit opinion does not establish financial health; highlight adverse audit information when present.
- Do not declare the company safe, unsafe, approved, or rejected.
- End with evidence-based follow-up questions or matters to investigate, without inventing explanations.

Return only the summary in Markdown, with no preamble or code fence.

Additional constraints from the first review:
- Target 120 words, with an absolute maximum of 150; use three compact paragraphs without headings.
- Count shareholders from the shareholders list, never from the directors list; omit counts if unnecessary.
- Incorporation state does not establish the location of all operations.
- Retaining the same audit firm alone is not evidence of an independence concern.
- Do not suggest governance breaches or issues with appointment dates without supporting evidence.
- For negative free cash flow across all years, say it remained negative, not that it turned negative.
- Follow-up questions must directly reference an observed concern; do not invent its cause.
